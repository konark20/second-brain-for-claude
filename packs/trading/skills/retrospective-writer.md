# Retrospective Writer

## When NOT to use

- Mid-period reviews (session-cadence weekly mode or performance-analysis)
- Single-trade postmortems (trade-analysis)


## Purpose

Compiles an end-of-period retrospective from the tracker, scorecards and journal: what happened, what was decided and why, which decision types made or lost money, and what changes next period.


## Procedure

1. Plan vs actual: the original plan's sleeves and budgets versus what actually ran. Breaches listed plainly, including profitable ones; drift is drift even when it pays
2. Performance: time-weighted return versus the benchmark for the window, the capital path with peak and trough, attribution by sleeve and by decision type
3. Decision-type attribution is the centrepiece: group every trade by the kind of decision behind it (dated-catalyst defined-risk spreads, liquid directional calls, illiquid story trades, rule overrides) and report the hit rate and P&L of each. Count structure errors caught before trading by payoff-check
4. Lessons log: lift the tracker's contemporaneous lessons section where possible; notes written at the time read more honestly than reconstruction
5. Tools: which broker tools were actually used versus planned
6. One page at most on what changes next period, each item traceable to a logged event


## Style rules

- research-documentation skill governs tone: human, specific, no AI tells, no em dashes
- Numbers from the tracker, not memory
- The honest version outperforms the flattering version with judges who watched the account daily
